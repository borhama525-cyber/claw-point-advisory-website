Echo Method

Down-payment funding for your end buyer on the back half of a double close, repaid from the deal’s spread on the same settlement. One closing, and everyone is paid at the table.

Echo Calculator

What the Echo Method is

Echo is short-term funding for the end buyer’s down payment and closing costs on the second leg of a double close. The end buyer closes with their primary lender plus our capital, and we are repaid out of the deal’s spread on that same settlement statement. The money goes in and comes back out at one closing: that is the echo.

Unlike a gap loan, nothing is left recorded against the property and no second-position debt sits on the deal for months. Every party (seller, wholesaler, end buyer and lender) is paid at the table.

Who it’s for

Echo solves a problem for both people in the deal. The wholesaler has a real spread and a buyer who keeps stalling because they can’t cover the down payment; Echo turns “my buyer needs three more weeks” into a closing date. The end buyer, usually a renovator buying from a wholesaler, has a primary lender lined up but doesn’t want to drain reserves or take on second-position debt. And there is a third case: an investor on both sides, wholesaling a deal to their own buying entity, where Echo covers the down payment on the second leg.

The two legs and three funding pieces

A double close is two purchases back to back: the A–B leg, where the wholesaler buys from the seller, and the B–C leg, where the end buyer buys from the wholesaler at a higher price. Three pieces make the B–C leg close:

  1. 01
    The primary lender. An investor-focused lender, sized as a percentage of the appraisal-supported B–C price. It brings the largest piece. If your buyer doesn’t have one, we can provide one.
  2. 02
    Our down-payment funding. We wire the end buyer’s down payment and closing-cost gap to title.
  3. 03
    The spread. The difference between the B–C and A–B prices: the wholesaler’s profit, and the source of our repayment.

How the money moves

Everything runs through licensed title and escrow, in a strict order.

The primary lender goes first. Its funds land at title and are confirmed before our capital moves. Ours never leads.

We wire the down payment to title. Never to a person, and only once the primary funds are in place.

The spread repays us on the same statement. When the B–C closing records, title disburses the wholesaler’s spread with our repayment carved out, under disbursement instructions agreed before any money moves. Money in and money out on the same paperwork.

The test every Echo must pass

One check separates a fundable Echo from a hopeful one: the spread has to cover our funding plus our fee. The Stack Method is repaid through the seller carry; Echo is repaid through the spread. If the spread covers the repayment, everyone is paid at one table. If it doesn’t, it isn’t an Echo we can fund. Raise the B–C price (only if the appraisal supports it), reduce the funded amount, or restructure.

That leads to the second check: the B–C price must appraise, because the appraisal sizes the primary loan, and the primary loan sizes everything else.

Doing it properly: title discipline

An Echo is only as safe as its sequencing, so we never improvise it:

  • Senior funds land first. The primary lender’s money is confirmed at title before ours moves.
  • Our wire goes to title, never to a person. No exceptions.
  • Repayment comes off the settlement statement. Under disbursement instructions agreed before funding, not on anyone’s promise afterwards.
  • Title has to be comfortable with back-to-back closings. We can usually tell from one phone call.

When it isn’t an Echo

  • Buying directly from the seller with seller financing? That is a Stack. Run it through the Stack Method Calculator instead.
  • Spread too thin to repay the funding? Restructure before you submit.
  • Need capital that stays in the deal for months? That is gap funding, a different product with different risks. Echo is in and out of one settlement.
  • A B–C price the appraisal won’t support? No primary loan, no Echo.

  • A–B and B–C purchase contracts

Common questions about the Echo Method

Is the Echo Method legal?

A double close with disclosed funding, settled through licensed title and escrow, is an established structure. The end buyer’s lender must know how the down payment is funded, and lender and state rules vary, so have your own real estate attorney review your deal.

Does the end buyer need any cash?

Often very little, sometimes none, as long as the spread covers the down payment, the closing costs and our fee. The Echo Calculator shows whether your spread does.

What if the spread is too thin?

Then the deal needs restructuring: a higher B–C price if the appraisal supports it, a smaller funded amount, or a different structure. We’ll tell you the exact shortfall.

Echo or double close funding: what’s the difference?

Double close funding covers the wholesaler’s A–B purchase. Echo covers the end buyer’s down payment on the B–C leg. Some deals use both.

Does the end buyer’s lender need to know?

Yes. The source of the down payment is disclosed to the primary lender, and the lender has to accept it. We confirm this before funding.

What does it cost?

Each deal is priced individually based on its structure, timeline and risk. The fee in the calculator is an editable estimate; your written terms show the actual cost.

Don’t have a primary lender? We can provide one.

Many deals need a lender for the largest share of the price. Tick “I need a primary lender” in your request and we’ll bring one into your deal.
